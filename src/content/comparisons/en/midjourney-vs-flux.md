This is a comparison between a **product** and a **technology**, not between two similar tools.

**Midjourney is a finished product**: pay a subscription, open the site, describe something, get an image. Zero setup.

**FLUX is a family of models**: reached through an API, a third-party platform, or by downloading the weights and running them yourself.

That fundamental difference explains every other difference between them.

## Difference one: style versus precision

Midjourney has a **look**. It adds cinematic lighting, rich colour, and balanced composition whether or not you asked. That's why its images are recognisable at a glance.

This is simultaneously its strongest feature and its biggest limitation:

- **Excellent** when you want a beautiful, attention-grabbing image
- **Frustrating** when you want something completely neutral — a product on a white background, say

FLUX is closer to neutral. It does what you asked. Less automatically impressive, more precise in execution.

## Difference two: text inside images

This is the **clearest practical difference** between them.

FLUX is among the best available at rendering clear, correct **English** text inside an image. Posters, signage, and covers come out usable straight away.

Midjourney distorts letterforms badly enough that the result is usually unusable.

**But note:** both are **weak on non-Latin scripts**. The only practical workaround is to generate the image without text and add the type in a design tool afterwards.

## Difference three: cost

Midjourney is a **fixed subscription** starting at $10/month — paid whether or not you generate a single image. There's no free plan and no trial.

FLUX is **pay-per-use**: cents per image via API, and the Klein variant is completely free under Apache 2.0 if you run it yourself.

**Quick maths:** at 30 images a month, FLUX is far cheaper. At 500, the arithmetic flips and the subscription starts making sense.

## Difference four: barrier to entry

Midjourney wins clearly here. Open it, type, get an image. No variant selection, no environment setup, no licence reading.

FLUX asks you for decisions before your first image: which variant? which provider? local or hosted? Those are real barriers without a technical background.

## An underrated point: privacy

On Midjourney, **your images are public by default** and appear in the community gallery. Hiding them requires a $60+ plan.

If you're working on client work under an NDA, that deserves your attention before you subscribe.

## The verdict

**Beginner, or want beautiful images with minimal effort?** Midjourney.
**Need text inside the image?** FLUX, and it isn't close.
**Building a product or integrating image generation programmatically?** FLUX, the only option.
**Zero budget?** FLUX.2 [klein] is completely free.
**Professional?** Both — Midjourney for exploration and ideation, FLUX for precise final output.
