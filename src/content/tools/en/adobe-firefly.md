Adobe Firefly is Adobe's image generation model, and it competes on a different axis from Midjourney and Flux. Its images aren't the best-looking in the category — but they're the ones you can defensibly use in commercial work.

Firefly was trained on Adobe Stock and licensed content rather than scraped from the open web, and Adobe indemnifies enterprise customers against copyright claims. For an agency delivering to a client, that distinction outweighs a modest quality gap.

## What sets it apart

- **Trained on licensed content,** with commercial indemnification for enterprise plans
- **Generative Fill in Photoshop:** select a region, describe the change, and it blends seamlessly
- **Generative Expand** to extend an image beyond its original frame
- **Vector generation in Illustrator,** producing editable shapes rather than flat pixels
- **Inside the tools you already use,** not a separate app with a separate workflow
- **Text effects and templates** for quick design work without a full generation

## How to use it

1. Try it free at `firefly.adobe.com` with an Adobe account
2. For real work, use it inside Photoshop — Generative Fill is the feature that matters
3. Select a region, describe what should be there, and generate several variations
4. Use Generative Expand to change a crop without losing the composition
5. Choose a Firefly plan at $9.99 or $19.99 if you don't already have Creative Cloud

## Tips for better results

- **Use it for editing more than for creating.** Removing an object or extending a background is where it beats every competitor
- **Generate three variations of every fill** — the first is rarely the best blend
- **Describe lighting and perspective** in fill prompts so the result matches the surrounding image
- **Never rely on it for Arabic text.** Generate the image, then typeset properly
- **Check your plan's credit terms** before assuming a feature is included

## Who it's for

**A good fit if** you work for clients or brands where copyright exposure matters, already use Photoshop or Illustrator, need to edit and extend real photos, or want commercially safe generation without legal review.

**Not a good fit if** you want the best-looking images available (Midjourney wins), you don't use Adobe software, or you need a strong free tier — 25 watermarked credits is thin.

## Limits and warnings

**Image quality trails the leaders.** Midjourney and Flux produce more striking results from the same prompt.

**Credits across Adobe's plans are confusing.** What's included differs between Creative Cloud, Firefly, and standalone subscriptions.

**Indemnification applies to enterprise terms,** not automatically to every plan. Read what your subscription actually covers before relying on it.

## Alternatives

**Midjourney** is the quality leader for original imagery. **Flux** offers strong results with more control and lower cost. **Canva AI** is the simpler choice if you want finished designs rather than raw images.
