HeyGen generates video of a person speaking — from a script. Choose an avatar or build one from a recording of yourself, type what should be said, and get a talking-head video without a camera, lighting, or a second take.

Its strongest feature is translation. Feed it an existing video and it produces a version in another language with the speaker's lip movements adjusted to match, so the result looks filmed rather than dubbed.

## What sets it apart

- **Lip-synced translation** into dozens of languages, including Arabic, from existing footage
- **Custom avatars** built from a short recording of yourself
- **Script-driven video:** change a sentence and regenerate instead of reshooting
- **A large stock avatar library** for when you don't want to appear personally
- **Consistent output** — no bad hair days, no lighting differences between recordings

## How to use it

1. Sign up at `heygen.com` — the free plan allows three videos so you can judge the quality
2. Pick a stock avatar, or record a sample to build one of yourself
3. Paste your script and choose the voice and language
4. Generate, then review the pacing — scripts that read well often need shortening for speech
5. Use the translate feature on an existing video to produce other language versions

## Tips for better results

- **Write for the ear, not the page.** Short sentences and natural phrasing; formal written prose sounds robotic when spoken
- **Have a native speaker check the Arabic** before publishing — translation quality is high but not flawless
- **Calculate your minutes before subscribing.** Credits, not the "unlimited videos" headline, are the real limit
- **Keep videos short.** Avatar artifacts become more noticeable the longer a viewer watches
- **Use it for scale, not for your founder story** — anything meant to build personal trust deserves real footage

## Who it's for

**A good fit if** you produce training or onboarding content, need the same video in several languages, publish frequent product updates, or want to appear on camera without filming.

**Not a good fit if** your content depends on personal authenticity, you need long videos (artifacts accumulate), or you film comfortably and only work in one language.

## Limits and warnings

**Credits are the real constraint.** Premium avatar video runs about 20 credits per minute, so the entry plan covers roughly ten minutes a month.

**Avatars are close but not convincing** under close attention. Match the use case to that reality.

**Be transparent where it matters.** Using a synthetic presenter without disclosure in contexts where viewers assume a real person is a trust problem, and increasingly a regulatory one.

## Alternatives

**ElevenLabs** handles voice alone if you don't need a face on screen. **Opus Clip** is the better tool when you already have footage and need it repurposed rather than generated. **Google Veo** generates scenes rather than presenters — a different job entirely.
