Midjourney generates images from text descriptions and is widely treated as **the gold standard for artistic quality**. Run the same prompt through it and through other tools, and the difference in composition, lighting, and overall feel is usually obvious.

But it comes at a cost, literally and figuratively: there's no free tier, and you have to learn prompt writing to reach the results you see online.

## What sets it apart

- **High artistic quality:** especially for stylized work, dramatic lighting, and composition
- **Fine control:** parameters govern aspect ratio, stylization level, chaos, and consistency
- **Large community:** you see other people's work alongside their prompts, which is the fastest way to learn
- **Continuous improvement:** quality rises noticeably with each model release
- **Character consistency:** features that keep the same character across multiple images

## How to use it

1. Create a Discord account (free) or use the Midjourney web app directly
2. Subscribe to a plan (starting around $10/month)
3. Type `/imagine` followed by your image description
4. You get four options you can upscale or regenerate
5. Use parameters to fine-tune the result

## Writing better prompts

The gap between an ordinary image and a striking one is all in the description. Use this structure:

**Subject + details + artistic style + lighting + camera angle + mood**

Weak: "a cat"
Strong: "a black cat asleep on an old wooden windowsill, golden hour light, realistic photography, shallow depth of field, warm quiet mood"

- **Name the style:** oil painting, photography, digital art, anime, 3D render
- **Name the lighting:** natural light, studio lighting, neon, sunset
- **Use parameters:** aspect ratio and stylization level change results substantially
- **Iterate:** the perfect image rarely appears on the first try

## Who it's for

**A good fit if** you're a designer or digital artist, need artwork for commercial projects, are a content creator wanting distinctive visuals, or are willing to invest time in learning prompts.

**Not a good fit if** you want to try before paying, need ready-made template designs (Canva fits better), want quick simple images without a learning curve, or need accurate text inside images (still a general weakness in these tools).

## Limits and warnings

**No free trial** — you pay from the start.

**Text inside images** usually comes out garbled, especially Arabic.

**Images are public by default** on standard plans; privacy requires Pro.

**Commercial use:** review the official terms, and note that AI image rights remain legally unsettled in many jurisdictions.

## Alternatives

**Canva AI** is far easier and partly free, but lower in artistic quality. **ChatGPT** generates images on paid plans and is simpler to use. If artistic quality is your absolute priority, Midjourney remains the strongest.
