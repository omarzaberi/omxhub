NotebookLM is Google's research assistant, and its defining trait is a restriction: it will only answer from documents you upload. Ask it something outside your sources and it declines.

That sounds limiting until you use it. Every answer arrives with a citation you can click to see the exact sentence it came from — which makes it the rare AI tool you can actually trust for study, legal reading, or research.

## What sets it apart

- **Source-grounded answers** with clickable citations to the original passage
- **Audio Overviews:** two AI hosts discuss your documents in a podcast-style conversation
- **Mind maps** generated from your material, showing how ideas connect
- **Study aids:** flashcards, quizzes, and structured reports built from your sources
- **Handles a lot at once:** PDFs, Google Docs, websites, YouTube transcripts, and pasted text in one notebook
- **A genuinely usable free tier** — 100 notebooks with 50 sources each

## How to use it

1. Go to `notebooklm.google.com` and sign in with a Google account
2. Create a notebook and upload your sources — PDFs, links, docs, YouTube URLs
3. Ask questions in the chat; every answer cites the source it came from
4. Generate an Audio Overview when you'd rather listen than read
5. Build a mind map to see the structure of a topic before diving into detail

## Tips for better results

- **One notebook per topic.** Mixing unrelated material dilutes the answers noticeably
- **Click the citations.** The habit takes two seconds and is the entire reason to use this tool over a chatbot
- **Ask comparative questions:** "where do these two papers disagree?" is where it outperforms reading them yourself
- **Use Audio Overviews for first passes** on material you'd otherwise never get to
- **Add your own notes as a source** so it can reason over your thinking alongside the documents

## Who it's for

**A good fit if** you're a student working through course material, a researcher managing a reading pile, a professional digesting reports and contracts, or anyone who needs answers they can verify rather than trust.

**Not a good fit if** you want general-purpose help, need to generate content from scratch, or want a model that searches the open web — Perplexity is built for that.

## Limits and warnings

**It answers nothing outside your sources.** That's the design, not a bug, but it means NotebookLM sits alongside a general assistant rather than replacing one.

**Daily caps bite on the free plan** — roughly three Audio Overviews and three Video Overviews a day.

**Citations reduce hallucination without eliminating it.** It can still over-summarize. Click through on anything consequential.

## Alternatives

**Perplexity** is the counterpart for open-web research with citations. **Claude** handles very long documents well when you want analysis rather than a searchable notebook. **Gemini** covers the general assistant role that NotebookLM deliberately leaves empty.
