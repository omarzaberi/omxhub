Opus Clip solves the most tedious job in content marketing: turning long video into short clips. Upload a podcast, webinar, or stream, and it finds the moments worth clipping, reframes them vertically, adds captions, and hands you a set ready to publish.

The part that matters is the selection. It looks for complete thoughts with a hook and a payoff rather than cutting every sixty seconds — which is the difference between clips people watch and clips that start mid-sentence.

## What sets it apart

- **Moment detection** that finds self-contained ideas, not arbitrary intervals
- **Automatic vertical reframing** that tracks the speaker so nobody drifts out of frame
- **Captions generated and styled** for silent autoplay feeds
- **Virality scoring** to rank clips, though it's the weakest feature here
- **Built-in scheduler** on Pro that posts directly to your channels
- **Editor export** to Premiere and CapCut when you want to finish by hand

## How to use it

1. Upload a long video or paste a YouTube link at `opus.pro`
2. Let it process — cost is based on source length, not output count
3. Review every clip; the virality score is a hint, not a verdict
4. Fix the captions, especially Arabic ones, before publishing
5. Schedule from inside the tool or export to your editor for polish

## Tips for better results

- **Feed it content with clear moments.** Interviews and talks clip beautifully; rambling monologues don't
- **Ignore the virality score** and watch the first three seconds of each clip yourself — that's what decides performance
- **Correct Arabic captions manually.** Wrong captions read as careless, and viewers notice
- **Batch your uploads** so you're not paying credits for the same source twice
- **Publish the top three, not all fifteen.** Volume without quality trains the algorithm against you

## Who it's for

**A good fit if** you produce podcasts, webinars, or long YouTube videos, need constant short-form content, run social for a brand with a video archive, or want to repurpose without hiring an editor.

**Not a good fit if** you don't already have long-form video (it repurposes, it doesn't create), you need precise creative control over every cut, or you publish only occasionally — the credit model rewards volume.

## Limits and warnings

**Credits are charged on input length,** not on how many clips you get. A long source burns credits whether the output is good or not.

**Free exports vanish after three days** and carry a watermark. It's a trial, not a tier.

**Always review before publishing.** It will occasionally clip a moment that's incoherent without context, or worse, one that misrepresents what was said.

## Alternatives

**Kling** and **Runway** generate video rather than repurposing it — different problem entirely. **HeyGen** is the better tool if you need a presenter on camera and don't have footage to clip in the first place.
